Inspect

Point at anything and see how it's built.

Click the PixelPeek icon, then hover. A small card follows your mouse and shows the size, font, colours, contrast, padding and corner radius of whatever is under it. No DevTools, no digging through CSS.

  • Contrast with AA and AAA, including text on gradients
  • Press F to copy it to Figma, S to save it
  • Hold Alt to measure the space between two elements

Components

Reusable pieces that keep your product consistent.

Colours

Select a section. Get every colour inside it.

Most tools only show the colours of the one thing you clicked. PixelPeek also reads everything inside it, so a whole card, hero or footer gives you its full palette.

  • Fill, text, border, icon and gradient colours, with how often each is used
  • Hover a swatch, click to copy the hex
  • Gradients you can copy as CSS in one click

Copy to Figma

From the live site to editable Figma layers.

Select an element, the visible area or a whole page, press copy, then paste into Figma. You get real rectangles, text, gradients, strokes, shadows, images and icons. They are layers you can edit, not a flat screenshot.

  • One click from the panel, or F while hovering
  • Groups named after the elements they came from
  • Tells you what it had to simplify, never silently

Design system

The design system the site really uses.

PixelPeek reads the variables the site's own developers wrote, with the same names and the same dark-mode values. Then it lists the colours, text styles, shadows, spacing and components the page actually uses. Nothing is guessed or generated.

  • CSS variables, with light and dark values side by side
  • Colours grouped by role, matched to the variable that holds them
  • Text styles named by where they appear: H1, Button, Link
  • Every button, input, card and badge variant, with its real values

Responsive

See the real mobile version, inside a phone.

Pick a device and the page appears inside it at its real size. PixelPeek also makes the browser act like that phone or tablet, the way Chrome's device toolbar does, so sites that serve a separate mobile page actually serve it.

  • iPhones, Pixels, Galaxys, iPads, laptops and watches
  • Mobile user agent and touch input switched on while it is open
  • Scroll the phone and only the phone moves. The page behind stays still

Private by design

        
Free, no account, and it never phones home.

      

      
    
        
  • Runs in your browser. Everything is read and built on your own computer.
    • 
        
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. No analytics, no tracking, no ads, no remote code.
    • 
        
  • Only where you click. It runs on the tab where you press its icon, and stops when you press Esc.
    • 
        
  • Your library stays yours. Saved elements live in your browser's own storage.
